# SOP-INV-001 Root Cause Investigation (v1.1)

## 1 Purpose

This procedure describes how the root cause of a deviation or laboratory out-of-specification (OOS) result is investigated and documented.

## 2 Scope

Deviations classified under SOP-DEV-002 and laboratory OOS results.

## 3 Investigation methods

Teams choose a method suited to the event: 5-Why analysis for simple causal chains, a fishbone diagram for multi-factor events and fault tree analysis for equipment or system failures. The method and the evidence reviewed are recorded.

## 4 Evidence and root cause statement

The root cause statement must be supported by evidence such as records, interviews, equipment logs or testing. 'Operator error' alone is not an acceptable root cause; the underlying reason, for example unclear instructions or missing training, must be identified.

## 5 Laboratory OOS investigations

Phase 1 reviews the laboratory for assignable cause (calculation, dilution, instrument or analyst error). Phase 2 is a full investigation if no assignable cause is found. Retesting follows the pre-defined plan and may not be used to test into compliance.

## 6 Root cause categories

Each root cause is assigned a category: equipment, procedure, training, material, environment, method or supplier. Categories are used for trending.

## 7 Precedent review

Investigators review closed deviations with the same event type or equipment and record whether the earlier CAPA was effective.
